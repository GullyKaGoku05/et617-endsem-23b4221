    Welcome to LaunchLab!

    In this short lab you will launch virtual projectiles and find out for yourself how the launch angle and speed decide how far something travels.

    💛 There are no wrong experiments here. Every launch, even an unexpected one, gives you useful evidence. Take it one step at a time.

    🌍 What is projectile motion?

    When you throw a ball, kick a football or fire a water jet, the object follows a curved path. It moves sideways at a steady speed while gravity pulls it down. The distance it covers along the ground is called the horizontal range.

    🎯 By the end you will be able to

    1. Predict how changing launch angle or speed affects the horizontal range.
    2. Compare the ranges of at least three angles at the same speed.
    3. Explain why 45° gives the maximum range and why 30° and 60° give equal ranges.

        Step 5 · Understand why
        
        The physics behind your results
        
        
        You already found these patterns yourself. Here is why they happen.
        
      
        

      
        
        
        
          
        1 · Angle shares the speed
        
          
        The launch speed splits into a sideways part (u cos θ) and an upward part (u sin θ). A bigger angle gives more time in the air but less sideways speed.
        
          
        R = u² sin(2θ) / g
        
        
        

        
        
          
        2 · Why 45° is the best
        
          
        sin(2θ) can never be bigger than 1. It equals 1 when 2θ = 90°, so θ = 45°. That is the perfect balance between time in the air and sideways speed.
        
          
        
        
        

        
        
          
        3 · Why 30° and 60° match
        
          
        30° + 60° = 90°, so they are complementary angles. sin(60°) = sin(120°), so both give the same range. 60° just flies higher and longer.
        
          
        
        
        

        
        
          
        4 · Speed has a squared effect
        
          
        Range depends on u². Double the speed → 2² = 4 times the range. Angle can only ever help up to 45°, but speed keeps increasing range.
        
          
        
        
        

        
        
          
        5 · Range is not the same as height
        
          
        Maximum height H = u² sin²θ / (2g) keeps growing up to 90°. Range is largest at 45°. A higher path is not always a longer one.
